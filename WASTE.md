# Eliminating wasted inference spend

Four places Quorum paid for tokens that bought nothing. This note records what
each cost, what changed, and how to see it in a trace. Numbers come from the
fixtures in `tests/fixtures/`, which are generated by
`tests/fixtures/_generate.py` and can be regenerated to reproduce every figure
below.

Companion to the prompt-caching work, and deliberately separate from it:
caching makes the same tokens cheaper, this makes fewer tokens necessary.

## The worked example

On **2026-08-19**, `deepseek-chat` returned an unterminated JSON critique in
round 2 of a live session. Three things went wrong at once, and only one of
them was visible:

1. The generation was paid for and thrown away.
2. The seat was marked absent for the round, so it raised no objections.
3. Its round-1 position — "run as-is" — survived to the verdict **undefended**,
   because the seat that would have had to defend it was gone, and so was
   every seat that would have attacked it.

The third is the expensive one, and it is a quality failure, not a cost
failure. This is the known cheapest-seat compliance concentration problem: the
seat least able to hold the format is the one whose absence is cheapest to
overlook.

The incident is now a regression fixture (`malformed_seat.jsonl`), recorded
with repair disabled so it holds the failure exactly as the pre-repair engine
saw it.

## Before and after, per failure class

| Failure class | Before | After | Where to look |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discarded malformed response** | 1 discarded generation (608 in / 86 out tokens); seat absent for the round | 0 discarded; seat recovered by local repair at no inference cost | `repair` key on the event; `malformation` in `session_closed` |
| **Debate damage from that drop** | seat contributed **0** objections; its position went untested | seat contributes **2** objections, text verbatim | `objection_raised` events for that seat |
| **Re-run after interruption** | restart re-bills every completed round — 1,256 tokens, **17%** of a full session, for answers already on disk | rounds 1..N−1 read from the trace; each round-1 sheet billed exactly once | `session_resumed` event names the round |
| **Development and CI** | 7,557 tokens per end-to-end run, ×N iterations, ×4 vendors | **0** — the full pipeline runs from recorded fixtures with sockets blocked | `tests/test_offline_pipeline.py` |

The percentages are of a small synthetic fixture and are not a forecast for a
production debate. The shapes generalise; the magnitudes do not. On real
traces the interrupted-run figure is much larger, because the arbiter's single
verdict call is over half of a session's input tokens and sits at the end,
where an interruption is most likely to have already paid for everything else.

## What each change does, and what it refuses to do

**Repair before drop.** Closes unterminated JSON, drops trailing commas,
escapes quotes a model left unescaped. Syntactic only. It never completes a
sentence, fills a missing field, or infers a claim — a repaired object that
read as complete but had been finished by the engine would be a fabricated
argument attributed to a real model. Where truncation cost a trailing
fragment, the fragment is dropped rather than guessed, and the trace says so.
Every repair is recorded and the report discloses it; `repair_json=False`
restores the old behaviour exactly and is how the control runs are recorded.

**Constrained output.** Where a provider can enforce a schema, it is asked to
— forced tool use on Anthropic, `json_schema` on OpenAI, JSON mode on DeepSeek
(the seat that failed). The schemas restate the contract `sheets.py` already
enforces; they do not extend it, and the parser remains the authority
regardless of what the provider promised. Prompt text is unchanged, so the
cacheable prefix is untouched and a provider without schema support behaves
exactly as before. Feature-detected: an adapter that does not advertise
`schema_mode` is called through the original one-method signature.

**Checkpoint and resume.** The canonical trace is the checkpoint store — there
is no parallel format to drift out of step with it. Resume runs under the
recorded session id, which is what makes the continued rounds' prompts
byte-identical: blinding labels and shield fence nonces both derive from it.
Resuming a finished session is a no-op that calls no models; a lock stops two
workers each re-running the remaining rounds.

**Trace-mocked development.** A recorded session is served back to the engine
as a provider, so the whole pipeline runs offline. Fixtures are synthetic and
generated, so they carry no secrets by construction and a refresh shows up in
review as a behavioural diff rather than a wall of new timestamps.

## Honesty

Repaired, retried, absent, and resumed are all visible in the trace and
reportable. Nothing here patches a response quietly: a repair that left no
trace would be indistinguishable from the model having got it right, and those
two must never look the same.
